TWO WAYS

Learn it once. Or hand it over.

VANS puts a ring on the next control and waits. You do the clicking, so the next time you know where it is. It moves on when the window shows you did the step. Leave the app and the guide waits for you; come back and it is on the same step.

The same steps, carried out by VANS where you can watch. It shows the plan first, and anything that changes something waits for you to confirm.

Part way through being shown, you can hand a step over. When a step is typing something in, the guide offers ⌘↵ Do it for me, and ⇧⌘↵ for the rest like it.

DIFFERENT

It looks at your screen, not someone else's.

A video shows someone else's screen, often on a version that is not yours, with menus that have moved since.

A chat answer is a guess about a setup it cannot see.

VANS looks at the window you have open, so it points at the button that is actually there. And it waits while you press it.

CONTROL

Nothing changes until you hold the key.

It asks first.

Anything that changes a file, a setting or an app waits for you to hold Return. A press is not enough.

It can be undone.

File moves and typed text go into a record, and each can be put back.

It does not open or switch apps on its own.

If a step needs another app, opening it is a step it shows you.

You set the limits.

Places it must never touch, things it must always ask about.

PRIVACY

Plain about what leaves your Mac.

VANS looks at your screen only when you ask it something, and only at what the question is about. In the test builds, your request and the text it needs go to the VANS server, which passes them to a model provider. The server keeps a short record of what you asked, and nothing from your screen. The panel tells you when text from your screen was sent.

VANS also notes which buttons you click, so it can offer what you do at the same time each day. It never notes what you type, and the notes stay on your Mac. In the test builds, a summary of the habits it found goes to the VANS server. One switch turns it off.

WHY

It started in internship season. I was looking at a long list of IDs on a results page, no names, and I wanted the ones that started with mine marked. Nothing could do that in the browser without setting something up first. I could have downloaded the list and filtered it in a spreadsheet, but that was a lot of work for something so small, so I didn't. What I wanted was to tell something on my screen what I meant, and have it done right there.

That is still the idea, only wider. Most of what I get stuck on is not on a web page. It is a setting in an app, an export option, a menu that moved in the last update. Most apps have no help that can see what you are looking at, and the ones that do each want you to sign up for their own. So VANS sits over all of them, and looks at your screen when you ask.

It can do the job for you, but what I care about more is that it shows you, so the next time you just know.

— Vedansh

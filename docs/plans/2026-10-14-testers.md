# Getting VANS to its first testers

The plan for the first tester build: what is decided, what is built in which
order, and what the privacy page has to say at each step. Accounts, keys and
costs are in `docs/infrastructure.md`; this file does not repeat them.

## Decided

- **VANS stays closed source.** Testers get a built app, never the code.
- **Testers use Vedansh's keys, through the VANS server.** The app carries no
  key and none of the instructions given to the model. A tester cannot learn
  how VANS works from the app.
- **Each tester has an invite code** tied to their name, with a daily cap. A
  code can be switched off at once. A total cap across all testers sits below
  the free limits, so one heavy tester cannot use up everyone's share.
- **Feedback goes to the server and is mailed to Vedansh.** Two ways in: a
  "send this failed guide" button and a "tell Vedansh" box. Both show the
  tester exactly what is sent before it goes.
- **VANS starts at login** and **sits in the notch** (top centre on Macs
  without one).
- **Watching and learning is in the first tester build, on by default.** The
  tester can switch it off; see below.
- **Only model providers that need no card.** See the table below.

## Watching and learning

The point is to notice what a person does again and again, and offer to teach
it or do it. It suggests; it never acts on its own.

**On by default** (changed by Vedansh on 2026-10-15, was off by default). Setup
says so on first launch, in one line with the switch beside it, before the
first request. Switching it off stops noting at once and offers to clear what
was noted.

**Rules**

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| On by default, said plainly in Setup with a switch beside it; the dot shows it is on; a pause from the panel | They know, and can always stop it |
| It notes clicks only: the app, and the role and label of what was clicked ("Mail, button, Archive") | Enough to find patterns |
| Only controls: buttons, menu items, menu bar items, checkboxes, radio buttons, pop-up buttons, tabs. Never rows, cells, text, links or anything inside a web page | Those labels carry people's content: an email subject, a file name, a page |
| Never typed text, passwords, window contents, screenshots or keystrokes; nothing in password managers; nothing VANS itself does while guiding or driving | Nothing private is kept, and no "receive keystrokes" permission is asked for |
| The raw notes stay on the Mac, are kept for seven days, and can be seen and cleared in Setup | The minute-by-minute record never leaves the Mac |
| For testers, what VANS learned (each habit found, and how it was answered) goes to the VANS server; Setup says so beside the switch. It may become Mac-only later (Vedansh, 2026-10-15) | Vedansh can see what VANS learns and improve it; nothing leaves without the person knowing |
| Learned from clicks the shell already sees and the accessibility permission VANS already has | No new permission prompt |

**Nothing is sent to a model.** The plan was to ask the model to name a found
repeat. Not needed for the first build: the suggestion is written from the
labels themselves ("In Mail: Archive, then Next"), and saving it makes a
routine whose instruction is those steps in words. So watching costs no calls
and sends nothing; running a saved routine is an ordinary request.

**Finding repeats** is plain counting on the Mac. The notes are split into
stretches at a pause of more than two minutes. A run of three to eight clicks
that comes back in the same order three times or more, on at least two
separate occasions, is a repeat; the longest one wins over the shorter ones
inside it. A run of one click over and over (Next, Next, Next) is not one.
Looked for after every twentieth note, and at most every five minutes.

**Suggesting.** A found repeat puts a small mark on the dot. Opening the panel
shows it until one is chosen: "You often do this. In Mail: Archive, then
Next", with Save as a routine, Show me, and Not now. Not now means never for
that repeat. A saved
one is a routine like any other, listed and forgotten the same way (that is
its undo).

**Timing: learning when** (asked by Vedansh on 2026-10-15). Beyond click
sequences, VANS learns when things happen and offers them at that moment: "you
usually start a timer when you open Mail", "around 9:05 on weekdays you open
Spotify", "the first thing after you open your Mac".

- **Also noted, on the Mac:** which app comes to the front, and when; when the
  Mac wakes, unlocks, locks and sleeps. App names only, never window titles.
- **Three kinds of timing, all plain counting:** at a time of day (the same
  thing within twenty minutes of the same time on three days of the last
  seven); after the Mac is opened (within ten minutes of the first unlock or
  wake of the day, on three days); after something else (within five minutes
  of an app coming to the front, on three days, and after most of the times
  that app came forward).
- **Speaking up:** a small line on its own background, just below the dot so
  the notch cannot hide it: "Start your timer, like usual?" with Run and Skip.
  It fades after about eight seconds. It never opens the panel by itself.
- **Trust that grows:** Run and Skip the first three times it is taken up.
  After that the line also offers Always. With Always on, the line says
  "Starting your timer in 5 s" with Cancel, then runs it as a request would,
  every confirmation included. Cancel turns Always off until it is chosen
  again. Skip three times in a row and that suggestion stops.
- Not yet: "before you finish work", which needs a moment that has not
  happened yet; a time of day covers most of it.

**Taken from Clicky** (Vedansh, 2026-10-15; how Clicky does it is in AGY G13):

- **Good morning.** At the first unlock or wake of the day, the line below the
  dot says good morning and what is usual today ("Spotify around 9:05, the
  timer after Mail"). Built from the habits VANS found, no model call.
- **Not while busy.** An offer due while the person is typing or clicking is
  held and shown at the next pause of a few seconds.
- **Snooze.** The line has Later, which brings it back in fifteen minutes.
- **Rest after dismissals.** Three offers of any kind skipped or ignored in a
  row and VANS stays quiet for the rest of the day.
- **After waking, and with the internet.** Nothing is offered until a few
  seconds after the Mac wakes, and an offer that needs the model waits for the
  connection to be back.

**Build, in order:**

1. Noting: the shell passes each click's position to the engine, which reads
   what is there, keeps it if it passes the rules, and drops notes older than
   seven days. The switch in Setup, "See what VANS noted", and Clear.
2. Finding repeats, with tests on made-up note streams.
3. The suggestion: the dot's mark, the card in the panel, save, show, dismiss.
4. Timing: noting app switches and the Mac's moments, finding when, the line
   below the dot, Run / Skip / Always with the countdown.
5. The privacy page text, drafted for Vedansh to approve.

## Choosing a provider

Every provider is used, and each request goes to the one best placed for it.

| Kind of request | Wants | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Quick choices | the fastest provider that is reliable at it | which application |
| Writing a guide or a step | the most accurate provider that is free right now | routing a request, the plan, the next step, the closing check |
| Looking at a window | a provider whose model takes pictures and is accurate at finding things in them | applications that publish nothing |

- **Accuracy is measured, not assumed.** A fixed set of real requests with
  known right answers is run against every provider and model; the score
  decides which kinds of request each may take. A provider that scores below
  the floor for a kind is never given it, however fast it is. The hands-on
  suite is the final check.
- **Speed is learned as it runs.** The server keeps each provider's recent
  answer times and failures, and prefers the faster of two that are accurate
  enough.
- **Busy or used up is skipped.** A provider that is overloaded, or has spent
  its daily allowance, is skipped until it is likely back, without waiting on
  it first.

## Applications that publish nothing

VANS reads a window through what the application publishes to macOS
accessibility. Most do. Some draw their own interface and publish almost
nothing; for those VANS looks at a picture of the window and the model
estimates where things are (D-169), which is slow and can point at the wrong
place, and doing the task is turned off because a wrong click there costs
someone's work.

What would make these work properly, most exact first:

1. **What they do publish.** Menus are usually readable even when the window
   is not, and many tasks start in a menu. Keyboard shortcuts are exact.
2. **The words on screen, found exactly.** macOS can find every piece of text
   in a picture with its exact place. The model then only has to choose which
   label, which any fast provider can do, and the pointer lands exactly on it.
   This uses text recognition that is part of macOS, run on the Mac. It is not
   a model VANS ships, but it is processing on the Mac, which the standing
   constraints avoid, so it is Vedansh's decision.
3. **A model that takes pictures**, for icons that carry no words, with its
   accuracy at finding things measured like everything else.

Doing a task in such an application stays off until pointing there is
measured to be right.

## The console

One private page, served by the VANS server at `/console`, reading the same
database. Only Vedansh can open it. Nothing more than this until testers ask
for something it cannot answer.

| Page | Shows | Can do |
|---|---|---|
| **Testers** | Each tester, their code, today's use against their allowance, last seen | Add a tester (name, daily allowance), which makes the code and an invite link; change an allowance; switch a code off |
| **Activity** | Each request: when, who, what they asked in their own words, which application, finished or failed, how long, which provider answered | Filter by tester, application or failed |
| **Feedback** | Failed-guide reports and "tell Vedansh" notes, newest first | Mark one handled |
| **Overview** | Requests a day, share that finished, answer time by provider, the applications used most, the commonest failures, each provider's use against its free allowance | |

What is kept for Activity is only what the tester typed and the result: never
the window's contents or a picture of it. The privacy page says so, and so
does the invite.

Until the console exists, a tester is added with a command on this Mac that
prints their code.
